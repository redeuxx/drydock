Creating a Google OAuth client for drydock

Drydock reaches Google Drive with your own OAuth client: rclone's shared client is being retired in 2026, and your own keeps your quota and your consent screen yours.

1. A project and its consent screen

  1. In the Google Cloud console (console.cloud.google.com), create a project (or pick one) and enable the Google Drive API.
  2. Under Google Auth Platform, set up the consent screen. Add the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file (or .../auth/drive if you chose full drive access).
  3. Publish the app (Audience: In production). An app left in testing mode loses its grants after 7 days, and drydock then shows the storage as "reconnect required". An unverified app in production shows Google's warning screen once; that is expected for your own client.

2. The OAuth client

Pick the type by how you reach drydock:

  • Web application, when you reach drydock over the SSH tunnel at localhost or on an https host name. Add this authorized redirect URI: {{ callback }} (it comes from DRYDOCK_BASE_URL). Then choose "a web application" when connecting.
  • Desktop app, when you reach drydock on a LAN address over plain http, which Google does not accept as a redirect. Google then sends the browser to http://127.0.0.1:<port>/, where nothing answers; you paste that address into drydock. Choose "a desktop app" when connecting.

Copy the client ID and the client secret into the storage's settings, save, then connect.

What to know

  • With the default drive.file access, drydock sees only the files this OAuth client created. If you delete the client and make a new one, drydock no longer sees the backups made before; keep the client.
  • Drydock stores the tokens encrypted, never shows them, and never uses a service account.
  • Revoking drydock's access in your Google account makes the next transfer fail, marks the storage "reconnect required" and notifies the failure targets of the jobs using it.
